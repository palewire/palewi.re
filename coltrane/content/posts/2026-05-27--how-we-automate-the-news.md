<p>I'm today's guest on the <a href="https://open.spotify.com/episode/3s7qE1CkCv2tgIW5b0cDMo">Data Journalism Podcast</a>, where I joined Scott Klein and Simon Rogers to discuss the state of our shared vocation.</p>

<p>I talked up how Reuters reporters are inventing a new approach to breaking news by automating coverage of news-making data.</p>

<p>As an example, I walked through how we attacked oil prices and public opinion polls in the early hours of the Iran war, a topic I addressed in a deck I presented at a recent Reuters town hall.</p>

<p>I've attached a few slides from that presentation, which show how we're aiming our arsenal at "the top of the file." Some metrics have been redacted.</p>

<img src="/static/img/how-we-automate-the-news-slides.gif" alt="Six-slide animated presentation about automating breaking news coverage at Reuters.">

<p>Later on in the interview, we dove into our industry's decline in open-source activity, a topic that Scott and I took on earlier this year with <a href="https://source.opennews.org/articles/journalism-lost-sharing-culture/">a piece published by OpenNews and Nieman Journalism Lab</a>.</p>

<p>We closed by unpacking our excitement about the rise of the so-called vibecoder, and our shared optimism about what fun may come when they get their "GitHub moment."</p>
