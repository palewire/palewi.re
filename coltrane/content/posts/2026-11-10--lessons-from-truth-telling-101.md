<p>Last night on East 21st Street, ten students gathered around a table for the final edition of “Truth-Telling 101,” the introduction to data journalism I taught this fall at the School of Visual Arts.</p>

<p>The six-week night class offered New Yorkers of any stripe the opportunity to learn how the city’s newsrooms blend cutting-edge statistical analysis with time-tested reporting techniques.</p>

<p>It was my effort to pass on the lessons I learned from the elders who inducted me into what one of them lovingly called “the empirical wing of the party.”</p>

<p>We didn’t cover any coding. In this strange new world, where a chipper chatbot can handle almost all computer programming tasks, the technical aspects of data journalism have never been easier to execute.</p>

<p>Where that leads, I don’t know. But I am betting it will lower barriers to entry and allow more journalists to spend more time struggling with what really matters: thinking clearly, asking better questions, developing knowledge and exploring the real-world significance of their findings.</p>

<p>So that’s what we spent our time on at SVA.</p>

<p>My lectures, our hands-on spreadsheet exercises, and the homework assignments are written out and open to anyone in my <a href="https://palewi.re/docs/truth-telling-101/">Truth-Telling 101 syllabus</a>.</p>

<p>While preparing the class, I assembled a database of more than 1,000 syllabi from data journalism courses all over the world. Sifting through it with AI helped me clarify my thinking and learn from more experienced instructors. Today, I’m releasing that index as a new interactive <a href="https://palewi.re/docs/data-journalism-courses/">app for exploring data journalism courses</a> on my website.</p>

<img src="/static/img/data-journalism-courses-browser-frame.png" alt="Data Journalism Courses app listing courses, instructors and schools.">

<p>Our time together was enriched by visits from three of New York’s best data journalists: Caitlin Ostroff of The Wall Street Journal, Haidee Chu of The City Reporter, and Bianca Pallaro of The New York Times. Their persistence, professionalism and attention to detail serve as ideal models for our work. I’m proud to share a vocation with women of this caliber.</p>

<p>Thank you to David Brand of WNYC and Sharon Lerner of ProPublica for submitting to an interview and serving as my poster children. You are living proof that you don’t have to be a full-time nerd to do data work.</p>

<p>And thank you to my students for six times submitting to my frequently folkloric and doggedly discursive style. The ones I can find on LinkedIn are Austin Steinhart, Macy Gilliam, NJ Smith, Isabel Díaz Alanís, Chrissy Wang, Tal Bogomolny, Yiyang Xu and Noelle Newbold. Follow them immediately!</p>
