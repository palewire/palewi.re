<p>In less than a lifetime, heat waves have evolved from sporadic, isolated episodes to massive, cascading weather events that can blanket vast swaths of the planet for months or even years at a time.</p>

<p>To continue calling such sprawling phenomena heat waves would be like calling a monsoon a rain shower. To understand the new reality of these immense expanses of heat, Maurice Tamman and I consulted with two dozen meteorologists and climate researchers – and analyzed temperatures for every hour of every day since 1960 for more than one million grid squares covering the entire globe.</p>

<p>From those results, we developed a new concept: the “heat storm.”</p>

<p>That’s the subject of a <a href="https://www.reuters.com/investigates/special-report/climate-heatstorms-science/">special report about heat storms</a>. We unpack our analysis and, with a team of reporters all around the world, tell the story of Vera, the biggest heat storm on record.</p>

<p>This is the first in a series exploring the increasing impact of extreme heat. As we’ve followed the data out into the world, it’s become clear to me that global warming is no longer some scientist’s theory about a far-off future. It’s a present reality that we can all see and feel.</p>

<p>Making sense of that unfolding story is what inspired us earlier this summer to launch the Reuters Climate Monitor, a live data dashboard that aims to track the slow-moving impact of climate change at the speed of the news.</p>

<p>Today, it expands to include a <a href="https://www.reuters.com/graphics/CLIMATE-AUTOMATED/MONITOR/akpeykqqapr/heat-storms/">new page focused on heat storms</a> that I put together with Casey Miller, where we will track their sweep and scale in real time.</p>

<p>You can learn more about our approach by reading <a href="https://www.reuters.com/investigates/special-report/climate-heatstorms-methodology/">Mo’s essay about his inspiration</a>.</p>

<p>I’ve posted the crucial portions of the <a href="https://github.com/palewire/reuters-temperature-storms">code and data behind the analysis on GitHub</a>.</p>

<p>So many people were involved in the assembly of this work that I won’t even try to name them all here. It’s humbling to work at an institution this ambitious. And I’m grateful that they let the nerds take a big swing every once in a while. That’s what drew me to data journalism in the first place.</p>
