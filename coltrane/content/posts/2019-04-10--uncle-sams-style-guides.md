<p>I filed a public records request with the U.S. Geological Survey for any style guides covering mapping and data viz.</p>

<p>I got back a ton.</p>

<p>Rule books, fonts, color palettes and even the Adobe Illustrator templates for the agency's cartography.</p>

<img src="/static/img/uncle-sams-style-guides-usgs.gif" alt="Pages from U.S. Geological Survey mapping and illustration style guides.">

<p>It's <a href="https://github.com/palewire/usa-style-guides/tree/master/usgs">all on GitHub</a>.</p>

<p>The most fulsome document is this <a href="https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/5816293-USGS-Illustration-Standards.html">illustration guide</a>, which I've published to DocumentCloud.</p>

<p>It includes examples of the agency's more complex data visualizations, which you rarely see in the news biz.</p>

<p>The U.S. Geological Survey's official sans serif typeface is Univers Condensed.</p>

<p>The official serif is Times New Roman.</p>

<p>Okay. Sure. The real weird thing. They have a custom font called FGDCGeoAge with symbols for geologic ages.</p>

<p>Want it? <a href="https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/2006/11A02/fonts/FGDCGeoAge_win_unix.zip">The TTF files are here</a>.</p>

<p>I also received similar guides from the Bureau of Labor Statistics.</p>

<p>I've acquired its data viz style guide via the Freedom of Information Act.</p>

<p>It recommends:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Sometimes making pies</li>
  <li>Avoiding 3D</li>
  <li>@YAN0's y-axis advice</li>
  <li>An internal chartmaker based on Highcharts</li>
</ul>

<p>Here's <a href="https://palewire.github.io/usa-style-guides/bls/data-viz/index.html">the whole thing</a>.</p>

<img src="/static/img/uncle-sams-style-guides-bls.gif" alt="Pages from the Bureau of Labor Statistics data visualization style guide.">
