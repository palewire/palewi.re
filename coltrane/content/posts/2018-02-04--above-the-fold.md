<p>Good morning from downtown Los Angeles.</p>

<p>The Sunday edition of the Los Angeles Times landed on my doorstep. There are only two stories visible above the fold. Both are Los Angeles Times Data Desk investigations.</p>

<p>I'm a little geeked about this. Let me explain why.</p>

<p>First, let's be clear: I'm an Internet nerd.</p>

<p>As you know, I spend too much time online blabbering about minor open-source software upgrades and questionable things we've managed to shoehorn on latimes dot com.</p>

<p>Our team is rarely focused on print, but I'm real proud of this.</p>

<p>Reason one: I love when computers help us tell stories that otherwise would not be told.</p>

<p>Exhibit A is <a href="https://www.latimes.com/local/politics/la-me-homeless-arrests-20180204-story.html">on the left side of the page</a>. It's a clever use of the LAPD arrest records that shows their rhetoric and actions on homelessness are out of whack.</p>

<p>Reason two: I love when data connects with old fashioned shoeleather to bring complicated issues to life.</p>

<p>You can see that <a href="https://www.latimes.com/local/california/la-me-drop-20180203-htmlstory.html">on the right side of the page</a>. It's a traditional government waste story made more powerful by linking it up with a huge database.</p>

<p>Reason three: I love seeing our young talent at the Los Angeles Times Data Desk stretch to do new things.</p>

<p>That homelessness story would not have happened without Christine Zhang.</p>

<p>She was a guest at the Los Angeles Times as an OpenNews fellow, where she proved her potential. Read her work. Then hire her!</p>

<p>Like any good reporter, Christine is strong willed about the things she knows.</p>

<p>One thing she knows is that the Python programming language, which I constantly preach, is a false God.</p>

<p>So this story was done in R, dammit. <a href="https://github.com/datadesk/homeless-arrests-analysis">Her code is available on GitHub</a>.</p>

<p>That story on the right wouldn't have happened without Ryan Menezes.</p>

<p>Ryan learned new skills to get a grip on tens of millions of payroll records.</p>

<p>As a result, he might now qualify for some sort of accounting license. And he's developed an earnest love for SQL sub queries.</p>

<p>There's a lot more I could say, but I should shut up. Check out the stories. As always, let us know what sucks.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, I think I'm going to find somewhere to have this framed.</p>

<p>It's the A1 editor's handwritten note yesterday listing Los Angeles Times Data Desk work in the top two slots.</p>
